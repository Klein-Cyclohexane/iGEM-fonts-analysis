Project Description

FIAT LUX leverages quorum quenching enzymes to disrupt bacterial communication in Dickeya solani, the causative agent of blackleg disease in potatoes. By degrading pathogen signaling molecules, we aim to provide a sustainable biocontrol alternative to chemical pesticides.

How Quorum Quenching Works

Bacteria use N-acyl homoserine lactones (AHLs) as signaling molecules to coordinate virulence factor expression. Our engineered E. coli strains produce AHL-lactonases that hydrolyze these signals, effectively silencing the pathogen's attack.

Key Innovation

Unlike traditional antibiotics, quorum quenching does not kill bacteria — it disarms them. This reduces selective pressure for resistance, making it a more evolutionarily robust strategy for long-term disease management.

Paragraph

In the field of synthetic biology, the design of genetic circuits often involves iterative cycles of modeling, assembly, and testing. The choice of a typeface might seem trivial compared to the complexity of gene regulation, yet it plays a crucial role in communicating your results effectively. Judges and visitors to your iGEM wiki will spend considerable time reading your project description, experiments, and conclusions. Therefore, selecting a highly legible font ensures that your hard work is presented in the most accessible manner possible, reducing cognitive load and enhancing overall comprehension.

Quorum Quenching

The enzymatic degradation of bacterial signaling molecules prevents the activation of virulence genes without imposing selective pressure for resistance — a key advantage over conventional antibiotics.

Blackleg Disease

Caused by Dickeya solani and Pectobacterium spp., blackleg is one of the most destructive bacterial diseases in potato production, with yield losses reaching 20–30% in severe outbreaks across European growing regions.

Sustainable Agriculture

With increasing regulatory restrictions on chemical pesticides, biocontrol solutions like FIAT LUX represent a necessary step toward sustainable, resilient food production systems that meet both farmer and consumer expectations.
